## Project overview

I designed a conversion-focused homepage for a high-consideration medical service — full-arch implant treatment. The goal was to create a modern, elevated experience that builds trust quickly, explains a complex treatment clearly, and guides prospective patients toward booking a private consultation.

## Design approach

### UX & user journey

The page follows the decision journey of a prospective patient researching an expensive, life-changing treatment:

1. **What is this?** — Hero establishes specialist focus and primary benefit  
2. **Is this for me?** — Problem section mirrors patient frustrations (failing teeth, dentures, anxiety)  
3. **Can I trust them?** — Trust strip, clinical approach, lead clinician, testimonials  
4. **What happens?** — Interactive 5-step treatment journey  
5. **What will it look like?** — Before/after slider and case gallery  
6. **What about my concerns?** — FAQ handles candidacy, pain, aesthetics, cost, dentures  
7. **What's the next step?** — Consultation form with clear inclusions and no-obligation framing  

### Visual direction

Rather than a generic blue-and-white dental template, I developed a warm editorial direction:

- **Palette:** Cream (#F6F2EB), deep sage (#3D4F44), bronze accents (#A68452)  
- **Typography:** Cormorant Garamond (display) + Instrument Sans (UI)  
- **Photography:** Close-crop editorial portraits — confident smiles, clinical environment, consistent case-study style  
- **Layout:** Asymmetric hero grid, generous whitespace, restrained UI  

### Conversion strategy

- Primary CTA: **"Book a private consultation"** — specific, low-commitment  
- Secondary path: click-to-call for high-intent mobile users  
- Trust before price — cost addressed in FAQ, not hero  
- Mid-page CTA after social proof sections  
- Sticky mobile CTA bar  
- No fake statistics, awards, or fabricated patient numbers

## Competitive research

I reviewed 10+ full-arch implant clinics (ClearChoice, Nuvia, Carolina Implants, Harley Street clinics, Perth Dental Implant Centre, and others) and identified common patterns to adopt (clear process timelines, consultation CTAs, before/after proof) and avoid (visual sameness, premature price anchoring, aggressive urgency tactics, generic empathy copy).

---

## What I'd test after launch

- Hero headline variants (benefit-led vs process-led)  
- CTA copy: "Book a private consultation" vs "Request your treatment plan"  
- Form length (5 fields vs 3 + callback)  
- Before/after placement in page flow  
- Mobile sticky call button vs form-only
